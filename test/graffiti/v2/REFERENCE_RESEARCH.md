# V2 evidence review — nine graffiti handstyles

**Status: research only.** Do not modify production tower or ship any V2 glyph updates until there are traceable reference observations. The first speculative `v2-families.mjs` was discarded. Last verified V1 release remains unchanged.

## What the sources can and cannot prove

**Primary source supplied by user:** offline saved Bombing Science article *Graffiti Letters: 61 Artists Share Styles* (2023). Its HTML contains **60 complete A–Z sheets** plus an incomplete A–L lead image. Refer to sheets by **1-based image index**, in article order. These are letter *studies*, not photographs of complete tags; visual notes below were checked against the original 60-sheet contact sheet and prior Phase C sampling. No source JPEGs are committed.

**Secondary sources:** actual photographed tags, curated writer archives, and first-person writer interviews. We use them to understand *whole-name* movement, repeated gestures, starting/ending terminals, marker/brush mechanics and connections. They are **not evidence that the artist who wrote one of our numbered alphabet sheets produced a particular street tag**. Do not invent such provenance.

Photographic/artist references:
- [Bombing Science — Top 50 Graffiti Handstyles](https://www.bombingscience.com/top-50-graffiti-handstyles/) — writer-identified tags; compare full-name balance and different writing styles.
- [Endless Canvas — Hand Styles](https://endlesscanvas.com/?tag=handstyle) — photographed handstyles in urban settings; compare wall size, tilt, empty space, terminal strokes and overlaps.
- [Endless Canvas — archived handstyle examples](https://endlesscanvas.com/?cat=12&paged=2) — photographed individual tags, with location/writer tags where available.
- [Bombing Science — Graffiti Letters: 61 Artists](https://www.bombingscience.com/graffiti-letters-61-graffiti-artists-share-their-styles/) — context and provenance of the user's offline A–Z sheets.
- [Bombing Science — 26 Letters of Style #8](https://www.bombingscience.com/26-letters-of-style-8/) — writer KENO describes fluent legible movement and avoiding unnecessary frills; SYHIS explains outlines and negative space in layered lettering.
- [Niels Shoe Meulman — original calligraffiti works](https://www.nielsshoemeulman.com/calligraffiti/) and [first-person interview](https://www.urbanartcore.com/niels-shoe-meulman-calligraffiti-isnt-new/) — distinguish real pen/brush/spray gesture scale and fixed-width-tool effects. A historical/calligraphic *comparison*, not a claim that the sheet authorship is Meulman's.
- [Cholo Writing research/book](https://books.google.com/books/about/Cholo_Writing.html?id=UPko0AEACAAJ) — genuine historical plaques/blackletter evidence, important to avoid treating Gothic merely as spikes.
- [University of São Paulo: pixação aesthetic](https://www.scielo.br/j/nec/a/CntcCWDqwGFNFFqLYTYvMRG/?lang=pt) and [Pereira: São Paulo writing](https://revistas.usp.br/cadernosdecampo/pt_BR/article/view/50255) — angular upright writing as a distinctive tradition; *not* a generic substitute for the uploaded angular handstyle.
- [GROG interview/profile: Nargue](https://graffzoo.com/en/news/scribblers-doodling-with-nargue-curated-by-grog) — complex repeatable full-tag anatomy that remains a word even when non-writers cannot decode it.

## Family-by-family evidence and research tasks

| Family | User's anchor sheets | Already visible in A–Z samples | Real-tag comparison needed | Not justified yet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Interlocked marker** | **41**, supporting 1, 17, 26 | Sheet 41 has pointed A/M, crowded R branching, large angle changes and crossing cuts. The K upper junction and triangular M aperture survive dense strokes. | Compare writer-identified wildstyle and marker handstyles for how **one or two accents** organise the whole tag; measure where exits continue into a neighbouring letter instead of giving every letter an isolated extra slash. | Randomly deleting all hairlines or uniformly increasing tracking: neither follows a documented complete tag. |
| **B — Arrow-cross (formerly crossed marker)** | **7**, supporting 9, 22, 42 | Source 7 is **fine and skeletal**, with open crossed nodes, circled/pierced counters, airy open A and horizontal-pierced O. Its later heavy arrow-cross implementation is not a direct match. | Find writer-identified street tags combining crossed ovals and heavy arrow tips. If those do not match sheet 7, **re-anchor or split** rather than claim the source shows heavy strokes. | Pretending sheet 7 itself specifies a fat-marker arrow-cross tag; it does not. |
| **C — Cut-wedge angular** | **13**, supporting 3, 38, 48 | Acute filled cuts and zigzag bowls; B replaces rounded bowls with peaks, K uses converging wedges, G uses a bar cutting the zigzag. | Compare whole-word directional balance against recorded angular writing, and inspect whether sharp triangles behave as *letter* anatomy or ornament. Historical pixação is a useful contrast, not the same tradition by default. | Arbitrary roughening/noise; replacing original wedges with stock block capitals. |
| **D — Rectilinear block** | **20**, supporting 16, 33 | Rectangular cavities, hard E shelves and almost squared D; sheet 20 has uneven handwritten shelf lengths despite boxy grammar. | Compare photographed block handstyles and separate them from outlined larger blockbusters; look for purposeful width variation and local marker-ended corners. | Adding random wobble or treating a fast tag as a mechanical stencil. |
| **E — Fractured Gothic** | **29**, supporting 12, 15, 34, 37, 39, 44, 51, 58 | Tall parallel cut strokes, thin secondary connectors, sharp folded flags and fractured bowls; the minims repeat strongly, risking I/M/H ambiguity. | Compare genuine historic blackletter/placa inscriptions and Meulman's brush writing to study **top shoulder connections, different stem groupings, and true tool-derived contrast**. Respect that these are distinct traditions. | Adding a hairline through every letter centre, or equating Gothic with arbitrary spiked fonts. |
| **F — Broad-nib calligraffiti** | **59**, supporting 4, 5, 25, 32, 46, 50, 56, 60 | Broad directional ribbons, thin loop entries, broken little hooks, sweeping exits. Sheet 59 is a handwritten multi-column sample, not a universal -39° nib formula. | Use Meulman's work/interviews to record how tool angle, shoulder-to-letter scale and wrist/body action change the stroke. Compare starts, tapering ends and selected *whole-word* connections. | Changing the nib angle or stroke weight based on aesthetic preference without comparing real mark traces. |
| **G — Rotational loops** | **8**, supporting 18, 28, 47, 57 (former kinetic J sheets 36, 40, 52, 54 are secondary research only) | Sheet 8 repeatedly uses entangled ovals; O is an outer flattened loop with a nested eye; G has a large orbit and a crossing bridge. | Compare full-word circular tag photos (including EARSNOT/IRAK as a distinct photographed example) to identify main gesture versus secondary echoes, and where long tails genuinely terminate. | Cutting every letter to exactly two curves: constant gesture count does not follow writing. |
| **H — Fragmented brush** | **30**, supporting 24, 27 | Red slash segments, abrupt detached high-angle strokes and white triangular omissions, with K formed by separated cuts. | Compare angular tag photographs for intentional pen **lifts** versus real rough edges; keep non-contact areas structural rather than image noise. | Uniformly deleting a fifth stroke from every letter or adding generic grunge. |
| **I — Angular chisel gestures** | **10**, supporting 14, 35, 53 | Cyan one/two-pass letters, slender upright/trunk strokes, small offset head cuts and very open counters. | Look for documented chisel-marker tags and compare angle, squared starts and sharp ends; record dry edges separately from letter path. | Inserting extra loops, thickening the entire glyph, or treating sheet 10 as interchangeable with broad-nib Gothic. |

## Before writing another line of V2 glyph code

1. For **each** active family select: its full uploaded A–Z sheet, at least one supporting user sheet, and a **photographed full tag** with source/photographer/writer provenance or a clearly labelled uncredited tag archive reference.
2. Make a small *evidence-to-mark* record: letter subset (A, G, M, R, S, φ where applicable), instrument, entry angle, terminal location, shared gestures, stroke width trend and at least one negative-space feature.
3. Distinguish the **observed** property from the **proposed** original transformation. Every letter edit must cite the sheet index and relevant photographed tag reference, or be labelled a hypothesis awaiting comparison.
4. Inspect fixed-word tests **PHI, φ, MUSHROOM, PROPORTION PIECE, SKUF**. Prioritise word identity and rhythm rather than comparing alphabetical tiles alone.
5. Version the work in an independent V2 test page; do not edit the V1 production family library, painter or historical tower atlases.

**Important provenance warning:** The article supplies A–Z images without reliable per-image writer identity in the archived HTML. Do not claim that an image-index number corresponds to any named artist. Artist-named tags from secondary photo archives provide mechanical comparison, not direct attribution to the user's sheets.
