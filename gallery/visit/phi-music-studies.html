Three studies in φ

These are original, algorithmically synthesised works made for PROPORTION PIECE. They are not performances of existing scores or samples of other recordings. The rule is audible in different aspects of each piece, and the resulting sound is not claimed to be intrinsically beautiful merely because of its proportions.

Phi Resonances — 89 seconds

The five simultaneous principal frequencies form a ladder: f, fφ, fφ², fφ³, fφ⁴. Their relative loudness diminishes by a related power of φ. Sections begin at 0, 13, 34, 55 and 68 seconds. This is a new composition investigating spectral relationships, not a recording or transcription of John Chowning's Stria.

Golden Angle — 89 seconds

Each pluck advances by the golden angle, approximately 137.5°. That phase selects a pitch from a constrained tonal collection and determines the note's left–right position. The next onset is varied by the fractional part of successive φ multiples. Harmonic colours remain deliberately soft.

Golden Measure — 89 seconds

The larger composition unfolds in 34 : 21 : 34 seconds. Its final section begins at 55/89 of the duration, approximately 61.8%. Shorter Fibonacci-derived intervals space the notes; an original harmonic event begins at the golden-section point.

Recording and reuse

All three recordings are synthetic performances generated solely from mathematical functions and original note choices. No pre-existing sound recordings, sample packs or protected musical scores were used. The gallery continues to play nothing until a visitor explicitly selects Play.

For reproducibility, see the audio-generation source code. The existing Debussy and Mozart recordings retain their own credited sources and licences.
